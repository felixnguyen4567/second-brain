# AI Vaporware

## Definition

**AI vaporware** refers to AI-powered products or features that are announced, marketed, and sold before they are actually delivered — creating a gap between customer expectations and product reality. The term encompasses "AI-powered" claims attached to roadmaps that remain unfulfilled.

## Why It Matters Now

The AI vaporware era is becoming legally and financially expensive:

- **Apple $250M Siri settlement (2026):** Customers bought iPhones based on promised AI features that didn't ship on time. Apple paid $250M for that gap.
- **Market maturation:** Users are more sophisticated, regulators are watching, lawyers are reading product claims, enterprise buyers are asking for proof.
- **Trust as liability:** When customers buy because of an AI promise, that promise becomes a legal obligation.

## Key Examples

- Apple Siri AI features (delayed, $250M settlement)
- General "AI-powered" claims used to generate hype, investor interest, and customer excitement without shipped capability

## The Core Problem

**Marketing outrunning the operating system.**

Companies were rewarded for attaching AI to the roadmap. Customers got excited. Investors noticed. Media amplified the ambition. Actual delivery could come later.

That window is closing.

## The Builder Checklist

Before calling a product "AI-powered," ask:

1. What exactly does the AI do today?
2. What is only planned for later?
3. Can a customer reasonably assume the feature already exists?
4. What happens when the AI fails?
5. Is the claim specific enough to be tested?
6. Are limitations visible before purchase?
7. Is there a human review layer where it matters?

## Key Insight

> "The demo is not the product. A demo proves possibility. A product proves reliability. The gap between those two is where trust dies."
