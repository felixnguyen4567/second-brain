# Orbital AI Infrastructure

## Definition

**Orbital AI infrastructure** refers to AI compute capacity deployed on satellites or space stations in Earth orbit, as opposed to traditional ground-based data centers. In 2026, this moved from science fiction to engineering pragmatism, with Google in talks with SpaceX to deploy AI data centers in orbit.

## Why Ground-Based AI Infrastructure Is Hitting Walls

| Constraint | Problem | Orbital Solution |
|------------|---------|------------------|
| **Land** | Data center permits face zoning disputes, environmental reviews, community opposition | No zoning in space |
| **Power** | Data centers consume ~1% of global electricity; AI demand growing faster than grid capacity | Continuous solar power (geostationary receives sunlight ~99% of time) |
| **Cooling** | Water/HVAC cooling is energy-intensive and geographically constrained | Radiative cooling to deep space (~0K ambient) |

## Key Players

- **SpaceX** — Only company with sufficient launch capacity + Starlink connectivity + Starshield AI integration
- **Cowboy Space (Aetherflux)** — Startup pursuing orbital data centers, raised $275M but struggles with rocket availability and costs
- **Google** — In talks with SpaceX for orbital AI compute
- **Blue Origin / China** — Other entities with orbital launch capability

## Competitive Implications

**Advantages shift to:**
- Companies with space access (SpaceX, Blue Origin, China)
- Companies with energy efficiency advantages (physics favors orbital radiative cooling)
- Companies with distribution (Starlink creates edge compute pathway)

**Disadvantages:**
- Traditional data center operators (Equinix, CBRE, Digital Realty)
- Nations without independent launch capability
- AI labs heavily dependent on third-party terrestrial compute

## The Deeper Point

> "The AI race is shifting from 'who has the best model' to 'who controls the compute substrate.'"
