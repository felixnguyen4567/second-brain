# India-Pakistan Ceasefire

## Overview

The **India-Pakistan ceasefire of May 2025** marked the end of four days of intense conflict — the closest two nuclear-armed nations came to direct combat since 1971. One year later (May 2026), the ceasefire holds but remains fragile and contested.

## Key Facts

- **Conflict:** May 2025, 4 days of intense fighting
- **Stakes:** Two nuclear-armed nations closer to direct combat than any time since 1971
- **Ceasefire:** Negotiated, held for one year as of May 2026
- **Fragility:** Pakistan's military warned of "stronger responses" to future provocations
- **Next conflict risk:** Analysts warn next conflict will escalate faster and be more dangerous

## Unresolved Tensions

The underlying issues that caused the conflict were **deferred, not resolved**:

- **Kashmir:** Disputed territory, ongoing insurgency
- **Water:** River sharing disputes (Indus Waters Treaty tensions)
- **Territory:** Multiple disputed regions along the border
- **Historical grievance:** Decades of conflict, Partition trauma, cross-border terrorism

## The Deeper Point

> "The silence after the storm is not peace. It is the space where the next storm is being prepared."

Deferral is not resolution. It is the accumulation of unresolved tension.

## Regional Context

- Both nations strengthened border infrastructure in the year since conflict
- Both deepened military relationships with external powers (China for Pakistan, US for India)
- Nuclear risk elevated — next conflict would likely escalate faster due to updated military capabilities
