## Core Story

Apple agreeing to pay iPhone owners $250 million over delayed AI Siri features is not just a class-action settlement. It is a signal that the AI market is entering a new phase where promises are becoming liabilities.

## Why This Matters

### Marketing Language Becomes Evidence
For years, technology companies have been rewarded for talking aggressively about AI. Product pages, keynotes, investor calls all leaned into: AI is coming, AI will transform the product. That worked while AI was still treated as a future-facing narrative. But once customers buy based on specific AI claims, the story changes.

### Apple's Brand Problem
Apple is one of the most disciplined product companies in the world. If even Apple can get caught between AI ambition and delivery reality, every company shipping "AI-powered" features should pay attention.

### The Hard Part of AI Product Delivery
A better Siri is not simply "put a large language model inside the iPhone." Apple has to solve simultaneously:
- On-device versus cloud inference
- Privacy preservation across personal context
- Latency acceptable for everyday voice interactions
- Multilingual understanding at global scale
- Deep integration with apps and system actions
- Safety around messages, calendars, files, payments, and personal data

## The End of AI Vaporware

Users are becoming more sophisticated. Regulators are paying attention. Lawyers are reading product claims. Enterprise buyers are asking for proof.

**For Big Tech:** Only market what the product can actually deliver within a credible timeframe.

**For Startups:** If you promise autonomous agents, AI employees, or human-level assistants, you must show boundaries, logs, review points, and failure handling.

## Key Lessons for Builders

Before describing a product as AI-powered:
- What exactly does the AI do today vs. what is only planned for later?
- Can a customer reasonably assume the feature already exists?
- What happens when the AI fails?
- Is the claim specific enough to be tested?
- Are limitations visible before purchase?
- Is there a human review layer where it matters?

Trust compounds slowly and breaks quickly.
