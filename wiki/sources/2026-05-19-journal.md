## Summary

Personal reflection on Trump cancelling a planned military strike on Iran (48 hours from execution) after Iran sent a peace proposal via Pakistan. Uses this as a case study for high-stakes decision-making:

**Core lessons:**
- **Sunk cost trap:** Treating original plans as living hypotheses, not commitments — new information should trigger reassessment, not defense
- **Last-mile problem:** When you've invested heavily in reaching a finish line, abandoning it feels like failure even when continuing costs more
- **Stopping ≠ losing:** Stopping is a resource allocation decision — redirecting energy toward better outcomes
- **Everyone recalculates:** Rational actors (opponents, markets, counterparties) constantly recalculate; the "bold surprise" decision is usually someone finally acting on what the data had been saying all along

**Applied to careers/investments/business:** Founder who keeps building product no one wants because 2 years sunk; investor who doubles down on losing position; professional staying in health-eroding job.

**Key quote:** "The ability to stop, reassess, and redirect is a skill that compounds over time. The people who execute it well are operating with a longer time horizon than everyone around them."
