## Core Story

Google is in talks with SpaceX to deploy AI data centers in orbit — marking one of the most significant infrastructure shifts in AI history.

## Why Orbital AI Compute Is Now Viable

The AI industry is running into a physical wall. Data centers are land-hungry, power-hungry, and cooling-hungry. Orbital data centers solve constraints ground infrastructure cannot:

- **Land:** No zoning restrictions, no permitting delays
- **Power:** Solar in orbit is continuous — approximately 99% sunlight at geostationary altitude
- **Cooling:** Deep space radiative cooling is essentially free at 0°K ambient
- **Latency:** Acceptable for batch inference, model training synchronization

Launch cost and hardware durability were always the sticking points. SpaceX has driven down launch costs, and radiation-hardened compute is now viable.

## SpaceX as AI Infrastructure Company

SpaceX is not just a launch company anymore:
- **Starlink** provides connectivity layer
- **Starship** provides heavy lift
- Growing satellite constellation provides distribution
- Starshield and AI partnerships make them an infrastructure player

## The New Competitive Landscape

**Favored:**
- Companies with space access (SpaceX, Blue Origin, China)
- Companies with energy efficiency advantages
- Companies with distribution (Starlink terminals)

**Disadvantaged:**
- Traditional data center operators (CBRE, Equinix, Digital Realty)
- Nations without space launch capability

## The Bigger Question

The Anthropic-SpaceX story earlier raised the issue from a different angle: frontier AI labs are becoming dependent on infrastructure they do not own. Google seeking SpaceX for orbital compute, Microsoft building Azure space capabilities, Amazon's Project Kuiper — the compute layer is being contested below the model layer where most attention lives.

## Implications

Orbital AI data centers are not the future for every workload. But they are the future for enough workloads that this story matters. The visible AI race is about models. The invisible race is about who controls the physical substrate that runs those models.
