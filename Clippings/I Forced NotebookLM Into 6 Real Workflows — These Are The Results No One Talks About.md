## 1\. For Content Writers: Turning Scattered Ideas Into Publishable Drafts

You know, most writers don’t struggle with writing; instead, they struggle with messy thinking.

You have:

- Notes from different days
- Half-written drafts
- Ideas you never finished
- Research saved everywhere

And none of it connects since it is too much, which the brain can’t handle at a time.

To research in the fastest way possible, I upload old blog posts, notes, research docs, Twitter threads, and even voice-transcribed thoughts.

And then I ask:

1. What is the core argument across all this?
2. Group this into 3 strong angles I can turn into posts.
3. Convert this into a structured article with a clear flow.

Here’s what this gives me:

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*P7bi1Vgs_TKH_9GV8mJ8qA.png)

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*bicnzei78dismhzRPyaGYA.png)

What next? I generate videos, infographics, decks, reports, and more right inside NotebookLM, and use them inside my content writing.

The best part? Instead of staring at a blank page, I’ve done a lot, from researching to getting visuals, and now I can start writing something real.

That’s the shift.

## 2\. For YouTubers: Turning Research Into Scripts That Actually Flow

To be honest, most AI scripts feel robotic because they’re written from scratch by an AI tool.

That’s the actual mistake that is coming in between your growth.

Good scripts come from structured thinking, not from generating via an AI tool.

Here’s my workflow:

I upload multiple research articles, competitor transcripts, notes on the topic, and YouTube videos.

Then I ask:

1. Reverse engineer these videos and tell me why they performed really well.
2. Turn this into a 7-minute video script with hooks, transitions, and clear sections.
3. Where would viewer drop-off happen?
4. Rewrite this to make it more engaging, not informative.

Based on that, here are the outputs:

## 3\. For Freelancers: Turning Client Briefs Into Actual Deliverables

This one is underrated.

Most freelancers waste time understanding what the client wants.

Instead, upload your client brief, past client work, brand guidelines, and similar projects.

Then ask:

1. What is the actual outcome the client expects?
2. Break this into steps I can execute.
3. Create a first draft based only on this context.
![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*U9PSFmnSip5HW3lHaqRo3w.png)

What happens: Instead of guessing, you get clear direction, a structured workflow, and your first version ready.

This cuts your thinking time in half and makes you look 10x more professional.

## 4\. For Students: Turning Textbooks Into Actual Understanding

Most students use AI to explain difficult concepts by writing some simple prompts.

That doesn’t work long-term.

Instead, upload your chapter PDFs, notes, past exam papers, and anything else.

Then ask:

1. Turn this into a step-by-step learning sequence.
2. What should I understand first before this?
3. What are common mistakes students make here?
4. Create questions that test real understanding.
![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*m403sMh2hTnZMf-d4nJgOQ.png)

And you get a learning roadmap through their “Mind Map” feature, clear concepts by learning through visuals or by generating a video, practice questions, and much more.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*PtbTThx1f3FsF8xGvbHMkg.png)

Thanks to that, you are actually not just reading.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*mCQhHbUMTrIAKqxnEMHRyQ.png)

You are actively understanding the concepts through different forms, asking questions you have in your mind, and grasping what you need to.

## 5\. For Founders: Turning Messy Ideas Into Actual Product Clarity

This is where NotebookLM becomes dangerous and helps you make money.

Most founders have ideas, but very few have clarity and a roadmap to make market-fit, build, make revenue out of it, and be profitable.

Well, that’s where you can add your presentations, docs, product idea notes, market research, competitor analysis, user feedback, and more.

Then ask:

1. What problem am I actually solving?
2. Who is this really for?
3. What is unclear or weak in this idea?
4. Turn this into a clear product positioning.

And you can get a clear target audience, a refined problem statement, strong positioning, missing gaps you didn’t see, and more.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*j_QoNwf4_R8TnaWod1LEUA.png)

You see, it forces your idea to become something concrete.

## 6\. For Educators or Course Creators: Turning Knowledge Into Teachable Systems

You know, I’m [selling digital products](https://nitinfab.gumroad.com/) on Gumroad.

And I know that educators and course creators are making a ton of money from selling products.

For sure, you can even create teaching material and sell online, but it is completely a different skill.

That’s where you can use NotebookLM by uploading your long-form content, notes, existing lessons, and more.

And then ask:

1. Turn this into a beginner-friendly teaching sequence.
2. Break this into modules and lessons.
3. Add examples and exercises.
4. What will confuse someone here, or how can I make it more practical?

## The Real Way to Use NotebookLM

If you’re using NotebookLM just to get answers, you’re missing the whole point.

That’s the beginner use case, and honestly, that’s where most people stop.

They upload something, ask a few questions, feel like it’s helpful… and move on.

But nothing actually changes in their work, because answers don’t move your work forward.

So instead of asking, “What does this mean?”, start asking:

- Turn this into something I can publish
- Structure this into a workflow I can execute
- Convert this into a draft / script / plan / system
- Make this ready to present, ship, or sell

Here’s the simple mental model: NotebookLM is for turning information into something useful.

But how? Well, turn:

- Notes → publishable content
- Research → scripts, reports, and assets
- Ideas → structured plans
- Scattered thinking → clear execution

So start pushing it harder and ask it to make decisions, fill gaps, create structure, and produce something you can directly use.

Because the moment it starts giving you something you can ship without overthinking, that’s when it becomes powerful.

That’s when it starts to feel unfair.
